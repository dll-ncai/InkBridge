\section{Notation, Data Budget and Statistics}\label{sup:notation}
\ext{Problem and Notation (Method); Datasets and Target Data Budget, Evaluation Metrics, Evaluation Protocol (Experimental Setup)}

Table~\ref{tabS:notation} repeats the notation of the paper so that this document can be read on its own, and Table~\ref{tabS:components} lists the components with the target labels and samples they use. Urdu (OUHD-L) is the evaluated target; the four source datasets are CASIA-zh, CASIA-en, HPL Tamil and IAM, and the six non-Urdu test sets are CASIA-zh, CASIA-en, HPL Tamil, IAM500, UNIPEN-de and OnHW (Table~\ref{tabS:corpora}).

\textbf{Target data budget.} ``1\% of Urdu'' means 1\% of the target training units: 336 of the 33,615 OUHD-L training units. Checkpoint selection on Urdu uses 1,000 labelled validation units. The script router is trained on the script labels of the 100,000 units of the joint training set, of which 20,000 are OUHD-L units (Section~\ref{sup:impl}).

\textbf{Metrics.} AIoU measures shape, strict order and Kendall $\tau$ stroke order, direction the drawing direction of matched strokes, and pen-up F1 the stroke boundaries; LDTW is used only for checkpoint selection. The definitions are in Section ``Evaluation Metrics'' of the paper.

\textbf{Statistics.} Every comparison is paired on the common units, using the per-unit mean over training and sampling seeds. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from a bootstrap with 2,000 draws that resamples writers on every set except OnHW, where units are resampled because it has two writers. Rows with fewer than three seeds are exploratory and marked by a table note. The selection tables resample writers on every set, so their OnHW cells rest on two clusters.

\section{Implementation Details}\label{sup:impl}
\ext{Implementation Details, Datasets and Target Data Budget, Baselines (Experimental Setup); Warm Adaptation, Script Routing (Method)}

\textbf{Inputs and frames.} The 64-px input re-draws the ground-truth ink with thickness 2, anti-aliased; a \emph{dataset} variant (the real image crop, area-resized) is used where marked. The 224-px warm-encoder render scales the ink by 3.5 with thickness 7. In $\Fm$ column 0 is set to background, which makes the shifted flip an exact involution on the stored image; re-rendering mirrored ink does not reproduce it byte for byte, so the stored image is flipped. Urdu ligature units are division v3.0 of the OUHD-L lines~\cite{hussain2026urdu} (Section ``Datasets and Target Data Budget'' of the paper); the other corpora are described in Table~\ref{tabS:corpora}. For connected-component units, strokes drawn as 2-px masks on a 64-px-high line canvas are merged when they touch; components smaller than 0.18 of the ink height in both dimensions are marks and are attached to the body they overlap horizontally. Checkpoint selection uses 250 validation units per dataset. The third-party corpora are used under their research licences and are not redistributed.

\textbf{Sequence length.} Each ground-truth stroke is resampled at 1-px arc length on the 64-px canvas, and the sequence length of 320 is fixed by the backbone. The step is enlarged only for units longer than 320 points (about 0.1\% of OUHD-L training units, 46.2\% of CASIA-zh).

\textbf{Backbones.} $\fnot{ur}$ is trained on 80,000 units (CASIA-zh, CASIA-en, HPL Tamil, IAM) for 100,000 steps (batch 128, gradient accumulation 4), seed 20261016, with a checkpoint every 10,000 steps; the checkpoint with the lowest pooled LDTW on 250 validation units per source is kept (step 60,000). $\fur$ is trained on OUHD-L only (100,000 steps; checkpoint at step 80,000 by lowest mean LDTW on the Urdu validation subset, ties to higher AIoU). $\fall$ is trained on all five training sets (100,000 units, of which 20,000 are OUHD-L units). Each backbone and each warm stage was trained once (seed 20261016).

\textbf{Urdu validation subset.} Checkpoint selection on Urdu uses 1,000 validation units from 31 writers, rows of the validation split drawn with numpy, $\mathrm{default\_rng}(0).\mathrm{choice}(4349, 1000)$ with $\mathrm{replace}{=}\mathrm{False}$, then sorted: 1,000 distinct units. The 354 units of 21 hand-corrected test lines are the gold set behind the segmentation F1 reported in Section ``Datasets and Target Data Budget'' of the paper.

\textbf{Target subsets.} The 1\% Urdu subset is the first 336 entries of one permutation of the 33,615 training units ($\mathrm{default\_rng}([20261016, 9])$), uniform over units, covering 183 writers and 305 lines; the 10\% (3,362 units, 247 writers) and 30\% (10,085 units, 248 writers) subsets extend the same permutation.

\textbf{Warm stage and fine-tuning.} The warm stage runs 40,000 steps at 512 sequences per step, encoder learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$ with 200 warm-up steps, other modules $8\times10^{-5}$, starting from the $\fnot{t}$ exponential-moving-average weights. The PE-initialised encoder loads PE-Spatial-S/16-512 weights with the patch embedding resized from 16 to 14. Fine-tunes run 10,000 steps with a checkpoint every 1,000 steps, selected on the target validation set by the $\fur$ rule, encoder learning rate $8\times10^{-5}$. Fine-tune training seeds are 20261034, 20261035 and 20261036; Tables~\ref{tabS:warm} and~\ref{tabS:singleR} and the warm-adaptation, retention and routed-adapter tables of the paper pool the three seeds, and 20261034 is the first seed of Table~\ref{tabS:singleapp}. For $\WFT{1\%}$ and $\WFTpe{1\%}$ the seed-20261034 run is an earlier run of the same recipe that predates the record of the pooled evaluation (Section~\ref{sup:rules}). Of the four 10,000-step milestones of each warm stage one is selected as the warm backbone: step 30,000 for the unguided warm backbone and step 40,000 for the PE-initialised and FM-guided backbones of the routed-adapter table of the paper (Section ``Exploratory Routed Adapter Results''). The milestone kept is the one with the lowest mean LDTW over the 1,000 units of the four non-Urdu validation sets (250 each; ties go to the higher AIoU); no Urdu data enters the selection, and the rule was recorded before the first warm stage started. For the unguided backbone, steps 30,000 and 40,000 differ by 0.00003 LDTW (0.27875 against 0.27878). The source-training control is selected by the same rule and is also its step-30,000 milestone, so it is matched in steps to the unguided warm backbone; the PE-initialised backbone has 10,000 further warm steps. $\SFT{\rho}$ continues $\fnot{ur}$ (model and moving-average weights, fresh optimiser) with seed 20261016; its 1\% fine-tunes use seeds 20261034 and 20261035, and its 10\% and 30\% fine-tunes seed 20261034. Sampling seeds are 0--2. The full recipe of every run family, the foundation-model roster and the baseline inputs are in the code repository (\url{https://github.com/dll-ncai/InkBridge}, folder \texttt{supplementary}).

\textbf{Script routing.} The router is a script classifier with four blocks of $3\times3$ convolution, batch normalisation, ReLU and max-pooling (widths 16, 32, 64, 96), dropout 0.3, a 128-unit layer and five outputs (Urdu, CASIA-zh, CASIA-en, HPL Tamil, IAM); it has 276,485 parameters. It is trained on the rendered 64-px images and script labels of the 100,000 units of the joint training set (20,000 per dataset) with AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-3}$, one-cycle schedule), batch 256, 8 epochs, random shifts of up to 3 px and seed 0; the epoch with the best accuracy on 250 validation units per dataset is kept (epoch 6, 98.3\%). Its five-class test accuracy is 99.6\% on OUHD-L, 98.0\% on CASIA-zh, 96.0\% on CASIA-en, 99.4\% on HPL Tamil and 96.4\% on IAM500; German belongs to no class. A router whose Urdu class is trained on the 336 units alone sends 93.5\% of the Urdu test units to the Urdu model, and 97.9\% with class-balanced sampling, against 99.6\% for the reported router (three seeds each; checkpoint selection uses a validation set with 250 Urdu units). $\RP{\rho}$ fine-tunes for 10,000 steps with each batch half target units and half units drawn uniformly from the backbone's training set. $\AD{\rho}$ trains one rank-16 adapter on the attention and projection layers. The two 1\% adapter baselines use rank-32 adapters on the attention, cross-attention and linear projections and the encoder $1\times1$ convolutions; one adds a shared tier and a temporal-pathway adapter, the other a rank-16 temporal-pathway adapter.

\textbf{Endpoint Consensus Selection.} Scores are rounded to nine decimals before comparison; the input-ink tie-break counts predicted points on the input ink after 1-px dilation, then the lowest sample index.

\textbf{Devices.} Training and sampling ran on cluster GPUs (two clusters) and one RTX 4090. DDIM sampling is not bitwise reproducible across GPU types, so every prediction file records its device, and each contrast records the device of both sides; for the plain comparator the cross-device difference is $+0.0004$ AIoU [$-0.0003$, $+0.0011$]. The comparator $\FT{1\%}$ of the routed-adapter table of the paper (Section ``Exploratory Routed Adapter Results'') exists in two sampled copies (RTX A4000, primary, and RTX 4090); the C-RADIOv3-H-guided adapter was sampled on an A30 (first seed) and on RTX A4000 (second and third seeds), so neither copy matches the device of the arm for all seeds. One 10\% PE-initialised fine-tune (seed 20261034) was resubmitted after a GPU fault; the tables use all three of its sampling seeds. Table~\ref{tabS:singleapp} gives the device check and the first-seed evaluation of the routed-adapter variants. On the first seed alone the PE-Spatial-B/16-guided backbone passes as well against the A4000 copy ($-0.0110$), and no backbone passes against the 4090 copy in either evaluation. No multiplicity-corrected interval is available.

\textbf{Baselines.} PEN-Net is re-implemented from the official code and trained on all 33,615 OUHD-L training units with rendered input for 100,000 steps (batch 512, learning rate $10^{-3}$, gradient clipping 0.2, seed 20261016); the checkpoint at step 100,000 has the lowest LDTW on the Urdu validation subset, and decoding is greedy. The Urdu-only 1\% model has the backbone architecture and is trained from scratch on the 336-unit subset for 10,000 steps. InkSight Small-p is applied zero-shot to the 4,406 test units.

\textbf{Example unit of the metric figure.} In the metric-illustration figure of the paper (Section ``Evaluation Metrics'') the unit is selected (highest $\FT{1\%}$ AIoU among the 62 legible units whose only error is one reversed stroke with a chord of at least 12\,px, writers of the qualitative-results figure of the paper excluded).

\textbf{Selectivity perturbations.} Each unit of Table~\ref{tabS:m1} is perturbed at five strengths (seed 20261016). Shape: Gaussian jitter of every point with a standard deviation of 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5 or 2 px, and a smooth elastic displacement field (Gaussian-filtered noise, filter width 8 px) with a maximum displacement of 0.5, 1, 2, 3 or 4 px. Order: one to five swaps of two strokes that are still in their original relative order. Direction: one to five strokes with a chord of at least 2 px are reversed. Pen-up: one to five merges of two consecutive strokes, and one to five splits of a stroke of at least four points at a random interior point. Timing: a sinusoidal warp of the time stamps with amplitude 0.15 to 0.75, which leaves the points unchanged. An entry of Table~\ref{tabS:m1} is the largest absolute change of the metric at the highest strength over the perturbations of the other factors, divided by the largest over the perturbations of its own factor (shape for AIoU, order for Kendall $\tau$ and strict order, direction for direction, pen-up for pen-up F1); perturbations under which a metric is undefined are left out.

\section{Foundation-Model Screen}\label{sup:fm}
\ext{Negative Results (Results); Warm Adaptation (Method)}

\textbf{Task translation.} Each FM is applied to the 224-px render; its token grid is resampled to $16\times 16$ where needed. Probe targets are computed per patch from the ground-truth trajectory: ink occupancy (shape control; IoU), signed local stroke direction in 8 bins (accuracy), stroke start- and end-point maps (R-precision), and normalised time rank (Spearman $\rho$). Ink models are scored zero-shot with the full evaluator, and vision-language models with four zero-shot questions (first-stroke direction, which of two highlighted strokes came first, whether marks follow the body, transcript error).

\textbf{Probes and screen.} A linear probe on z-scored features plus patch-centre coordinates (AdamW, 20 epochs, three seeds, hyper-parameters never tuned) is trained on a layer named in advance for each FM, with five writer-disjoint folds. The comparator is the best probe of the baseline encoders. An FM passes a set if at least one order target has a paired interval above zero against the comparator and the shape target's lower bound is above $-0.01035$. The Urdu screening set is the validation subset; five cross-script sets are added. An FM passes cross-script if it keeps shape on all five and order on at least three. The roster had 116 FMs in four families: frozen vision encoders, diffusion features, vision-language and OCR models, and ink models. The encoders behind the reported variants are C-RADIOv3~\cite{ranzinger2024amradio,heinrich2025radiov25}, DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3}, MAE~\cite{he2022mae}, the Perception Encoder~\cite{bolya2025pe}, CleanDIFT~\cite{stracke2025cleandift} and WebSSL~\cite{fan2025webssl}.

\textbf{Guidance.} The projector is a three-layer MLP (SiLU, hidden width 2,048, discarded after training); the alignment weight is 0.5. Ink models contribute pseudo-trajectories on unlabelled images, kept if the re-rendered trajectory has IoU of at least 0.80 with the binarised input. A guided variant is promoted only if its FM passes cross-script and the variant meets target gain and retention; it is FM-attributed if it also beats both unguided warm variants (AIoU non-inferior, order metrics non-inferior, at least one lower bound above zero).

\section{Full Decision Rules}\label{sup:rules}
\ext{Evaluation Protocol (Experimental Setup)}

\textbf{Margins.} Non-inferiority margins are 1.5 times the minimum detectable effect at 80\% power of a three-versus-three-seed comparison, computed from its standard errors on the OUHD-L test set (0.0025 AIoU, 0.0034 pen-up F1, 0.0025 strict order, 0.0030 direction): 0.01035 (AIoU), 0.0141 (pen-up F1), 0.01065 (strict order) and 0.01245 (direction).

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Target gain}: lower bound above 0 for AIoU, above $-0.0141$ for pen-up F1, above $-0.01065$ for strict order and above $-0.01245$ for direction, against plain fine-tuning at the same $\rho$.
\item \emph{Retention}: AIoU lower bound above $-0.010$ on every non-target set for the composed system against $\fnot{t}$; the three order clauses are reported per set.
\item \emph{Target gain plus shape retention}: target gain on the target and retention on all six non-Urdu sets.
\item \emph{Control comparison}: warm variant minus the source-training control $\SFT{\rho}$ on the target has AIoU lower bound above 0 and meets the three order clauses.
\item \emph{Selection gain}: at least one of pen-up F1, strict order and direction has a point estimate beyond its margin with the interval above zero (Holm correction across the three); this is a gain above zero, not a lower bound beyond the margin, no other order metric shows an interval-backed loss beyond its margin, AIoU is non-inferior, and the random-of-$k$ control has every interval containing zero.
\item \emph{Mirror collapse and repair}: collapse needs the direction change on a mirrored left-to-right set to have an interval upper bound below $-0.10$ on at least two of three sets per base; repair needs a direction gain with lower bound above 0 and AIoU lower bound above $-0.01035$ on at least two of three sets (three of four in the reverse direction).
\item \emph{Rank agreement}: Kendall $\tau$ lower bound of at least 0.65.
\item \emph{Frame-choice success}: OUHD-L direction improves over $\Fi$ with the same Endpoint Consensus Selection (lower bound above 0), AIoU is non-inferior, and the six non-Urdu sets keep AIoU above $-0.010$ and the order clauses.
\end{itemize}
The test evaluation of Endpoint Consensus Selection on plain fine-tuning was added after its validation result, under an amendment recorded before the test output existed; all other evaluations were specified before sampling. Test sets were sealed until each run's validation-based checkpoint selection existed.

\textbf{Stricter reading of the selection gain.} If the lower bound itself must exceed the margin, the gain clause holds for 4 of 5 bases on Urdu (not for $\fur$: lower bounds $+0.0116$ for pen-up F1 against $0.0141$ and $+0.0098$ for strict order against $0.01065$) and for 7 of 24 cells on the non-Urdu sets (recount from the unadjusted pen-up F1 and strict-order intervals).

\subsection{Negative results}\label{sup:frameres}
\ext{Negative Results (Results)}
\textbf{Mirrored frame.} In $\Fm$, image columns $c$ map to $W-c$, the trajectory is reflected ($x\mapsto -x$), and the output is reflected back before scoring. A backbone trained on left-to-right scripts then sees a right-to-left unit in its familiar orientation.

\textbf{Frame choice.} For each unit we draw $k$ samples in $\Fi$ and $k$ in $\Fm$. The frame score is the mean pairwise value of the endpoint-agreement function $U$ (defined in Section ``Endpoint Consensus Selection'' of the paper) over the samples in that frame,
\begin{equation}
S_f=\frac{2}{k(k-1)}\sum_{i<j}U\!\left(a^{f}_i,a^{f}_j\right),\quad f\in\{\Fi,\Fm\},
\end{equation}
and the frame with the higher score is chosen; ties go to $\Fi$. The output is the sample that Endpoint Consensus Selection selects among the $k$ samples of the chosen frame. No script label and no ground truth are used.

Table~\ref{tabS:r1d} gives the automatic frame choice against the identity frame, per base.

\textbf{Mirrored frame applied by hand.} On $\fnot{ur}$, $\Fm$ raises direction from 0.5468 to 0.8687 and Kendall $\tau$ from 0.4799 to 0.6586 ($[0.6204, 0.6931]$), with AIoU 0.7117 against 0.7090; the lower bound of $\tau$ misses the rank-agreement bar of 0.65 (Section~\ref{sup:rules}). On TRACE the same flip gives direction $0.476\to0.918$ at an AIoU cost ($0.517\to0.465$). After 1\% fine-tuning the frame adds $+0.0003$ AIoU (not significant). In the reverse direction, $\fur$ read in the mirrored frame raises direction on all four left-to-right sets ($+0.11$ to $+0.63$), but AIoU stays non-inferior on only two (IAM500, OnHW; CASIA-en $-0.016$ [$-0.027$, $-0.006$]), so the repair criterion (three of four) fails. For the warm backbone $\Fm$ raises Urdu $\tau$ to a lower bound of $0.419$, below the 0.65 bar; the PE-initialised warm backbone is not assessed.

\textbf{Other tests.} Four further tests did not pass their criterion: (i) direction guidance during sampling (best direction $+0.0017$ at an AIoU cost of $0.222$); (ii) renderer-robust fine-tuning (a gain on a second renderer, with order losses in all 20 configurations); (iii) a script-token joint model, which ignores the token (AIoU $-0.017$ against $\fall$); and (iv) 1\% low-rank adapters, reported in Section ``Negative Results'' of the paper.

\subsection{Metric selectivity}\label{sup:m1res}
\ext{Discussion}
For each metric we perturb the ink along its own factor and along the other factors (seven perturbation types at five strengths, 500 units per set, seven sets; definitions in Section~\ref{sup:impl}) and require the largest off-factor change to be at most 10\% of the largest own-factor change at the highest strength. Of 35 metric-by-set cells, 12 pass (Table~\ref{tabS:m1}). Kendall $\tau$ passes on all seven sets, AIoU on two, direction on three, and pen-up F1 and strict order on none. The two gated order guardrails, strict order and pen-up F1, therefore fail the check, and the only order metric that passes, Kendall $\tau$, is reported and not gated; a value of 0.000 means that no shape, direction, pen-up or timing perturbation changed $\tau$. The ratio of strict order exceeds 1 on every set: it moves more under perturbations of other factors than under order perturbations. We therefore read a strict-order gain as a gain in complete-unit correctness and a pen-up F1 gain as a segmentation gain, not as evidence about the relative order of strokes. The losses in $\tau$ at 10\% (Table~\ref{tabS:warm}) and for the routed adapter (Section ``Exploratory Routed Adapter Results'' of the paper) change no verdict under the pre-specified criteria.
